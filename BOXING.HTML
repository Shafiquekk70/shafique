BOXING

THE SWEET SCIENCE

Boxing is a combat sport in which two people, usually wearing protective gloves, throw punches at each other for a predetermined amount of time in a boxing ring.

BASIC INFORMATION

Match Format

Two opponents fight in a ring, divided into rounds of 3 minutes each with 1-minute breaks.

Match Duration

Amateur: 3 rounds × 3 minutes | Professional: 4-12 rounds × 3 minutes | Championship: 12 rounds × 3 minutes

Main Strategy

Footwork, defense, punching combinations, ring generalship, and conditioning.

Ring Dimensions

Standard: 16-24 ft square | Ropes: 3-5 ropes at heights of 18-48 inches | Canvas: 1 inch padding

BOXING STYLES & FORMATS

Professional Boxing

Rounds: 4-12 rounds

Duration: 3 min/round

Protection: 8-10 oz gloves

Scoring: 10-point must system

Amateur/Olympic

Rounds: 3 rounds

Duration: 3 min/round (men)

Protection: Headgear, 10 oz gloves

Scoring: Electronic scoring system

White Collar Boxing

Rounds: 3-4 rounds

Duration: 2 min/round

Protection: Headgear, 16 oz gloves

Purpose: Charity/fitness events

FIGHTING STYLES

Offensive Styles

  • Swarmers/In-fighters: Pressure fighters who stay close (Mike Tyson)
  • Boxer-punchers: Combine technique with power (Sugar Ray Leonard)
  • Brawlers: Aggressive, power-focused (George Foreman)
  • Counter-punchers: Reactive, defensive offense (Floyd Mayweather)

Defensive Styles

  • Out-boxers: Keep distance, use reach (Muhammad Ali)
  • Slick boxers: Elusive defense, shoulder rolls (Pernell Whitaker)
  • Peek-a-boo: High guard, head movement (Cus D'Amato style)
  • Philly Shell: Shoulder roll defense (James Toney)

RULES OF BOXING

Basic Rules

  • No hitting below the belt, behind head, or on back
  • No holding, tripping, kicking, or headbutting
  • 10-second count for knockdowns
  • Three knockdown rule in some jurisdictions
  • Referee can stop fight if boxer cannot defend

Ways to Win

  • KO: Opponent cannot rise within 10 count
  • TKO: Referee stops fight (technical knockout)
  • Decision: Judges' scorecards after all rounds
  • DQ: Disqualification for serious fouls
  • RTD: Corner retirement between rounds

IMPORTANT FACTS

History & Origin

  • Ancient Origins: Mesopotamia circa 3000 BC, Ancient Greece
  • Modern Boxing: 18th century England with Jack Broughton's rules
  • Marquess of Queensberry: 1867 rules introduced gloves, rounds
  • Olympic Sport: Since 1904 (St. Louis Games)
  • Women's Olympic Boxing: Introduced in 2012 London Games

Major Championships

  • World Titles: WBC, WBA, IBF, WBO (The "Big Four")
  • The Ring Magazine: Lineal championship recognition
  • Undisputed Champion: Holds all four major belts
  • Olympic Gold: Highest amateur achievement
  • Golden Gloves: Premier amateur tournament (USA)

"The Greatest"

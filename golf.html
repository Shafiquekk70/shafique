GOLF

A GAME OF PRECISION AND PATIENCE

Golf is a precision club-and-ball sport where players use various clubs to hit balls into a series of holes on a course in as few strokes as possible. It's one of the few ball games that doesn't require a standardized playing area.

BASIC INFORMATION

Number of Players

Typically played individually or in groups of 2-4 players. Professional tournaments feature 70-156 players.

Round Duration

18 holes: 4-5 hours | 9 holes: 2-2.5 hours | Professional round: 4-5 hours

Main Strategy

Course management, club selection, reading greens, mental focus, and consistency.

Course Dimensions

Standard course: 6,500-7,200 yards | Hole: 4.25 inches diameter | Par: 70-72 strokes

GOLF FORMATS

Stroke Play

Scoring: Total strokes

Common In: Professional tournaments

Goal: Lowest total score

Example: PGA Tour events

Match Play

Scoring: Holes won

Common In: Team competitions

Goal: Win more holes

Example: Ryder Cup

Scramble

Scoring: Team best ball

Common In: Charity events

Goal: Team lowest score

Example: Corporate tournaments

GAME STRATEGY

Course Management

  • Tee Shot Strategy: Positioning for second shot
  • Risk/Reward: When to attack pins
  • Club Selection: Choosing right club for distance/wind
  • Green Reading: Understanding slopes and breaks

Scoring Strategy

  • Par 5 Strategy: Going for eagle vs playing safe
  • Par 3 Strategy: Finding right part of green
  • Bogey Avoidance: Minimizing big numbers
  • Momentum Management: Handling good/bad streaks

RULES OF THE GAME

Basic Rules

  • Play ball as it lies (no improving lie)
  • 14-club maximum in bag
  • Ball must be played from teeing area
  • Hole completed when ball is holed
  • Penalty strokes for lost balls, water hazards

Common Penalties

  • Out of Bounds: Stroke and distance
  • Water Hazard: One stroke penalty
  • Unplayable Lie: One stroke penalty
  • Lost Ball: Stroke and distance
  • Grounding Club in Hazard: Two strokes

GOLF COURSE TYPES

Links Course

Coastal courses with few trees, firm fairways, deep bunkers, and influenced by wind. Originated in Scotland.

Famous Examples: St. Andrews, Royal Birkdale, Carnoustie

Parkland Course

Inland courses with tree-lined fairways, manicured greens, and lush vegetation. Most common in North America.

Famous Examples: Augusta National, Pebble Beach, Oakmont

Desert Course

Arid landscape courses with minimal grass, sandy areas, and dramatic elevation changes.

Famous Examples: TPC Scottsdale, Emirates Golf Club

GOLF EQUIPMENT

Drivers

Longest club for maximum distance off tee. Loft: 8-12°

Fairway Woods

For long shots from fairway. Loft: 13-21°

Irons

Versatile clubs for various distances. Numbered 3-9

Wedges

For short, high shots. Types: Pitching, Gap, Sand, Lob

Putters

For rolling ball on green. Various head designs

IMPORTANT FACTS

History & Origin

  • Origin: 15th century Scotland
  • First Rules: Written in 1744 (Edinburgh)
  • First Major: The Open Championship (1860)
  • USGA Founded: 1894
  • First Masters: 1934 at Augusta National

Major Tournaments

  • The Masters: April at Augusta National
  • PGA Championship: May at various courses
  • U.S. Open: June at various courses
  • The Open Championship: July at links courses
  • Ryder Cup: Biennial USA vs Europe

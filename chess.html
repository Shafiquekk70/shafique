CHESS

THE GAME OF KINGS

Chess is a strategic board game played between two players on a checkered board with 64 squares arranged in an 8×8 grid. Dating back over 1500 years, it is one of the world's most popular games, played by millions worldwide both recreationally and competitively.

BASIC INFORMATION

Game Format

Two players, White and Black, alternate moves. White moves first. Games can end in checkmate, stalemate, resignation, or draw by agreement/rules.

Time Controls

Classical: 90+30 min | Rapid: 15+10 min | Blitz: 3+2 min | Bullet: 1+0 min | Correspondence: Days per move

Key Skills

Tactics, strategy, positional understanding, calculation, pattern recognition, endgame technique, opening preparation.

Board & Pieces

Board: 8×8 squares (64 total) | Pieces: 16 per player (1 King, 1 Queen, 2 Rooks, 2 Bishops, 2 Knights, 8 Pawns)

PIECE MOVEMENTS

Major Pieces

  • King: Moves one square in any direction
  • Queen: Moves any number of squares horizontally, vertically, or diagonally
  • Rook: Moves any number of squares horizontally or vertically
  • Bishop: Moves any number of squares diagonally
  • Knight: Moves in L-shape (2 squares one way, 1 square perpendicular)

Special Rules

  • Castling: King moves 2 squares toward rook, rook jumps over
  • En Passant: Pawn capture after opponent's 2-square pawn advance
  • Pawn Promotion: Pawn reaches 8th rank becomes any piece (except king)
  • Check/Checkmate: King under attack/with no legal moves

RULES & TERMINOLOGY

Game Outcomes

  • Checkmate: King is in check with no legal moves
  • Stalemate: Player to move has no legal moves but king not in check
  • Draw: By agreement, threefold repetition, 50-move rule, insufficient material
  • Resignation: Player concedes defeat
  • Timeout: Player runs out of time (loss unless opponent has insufficient material)

Illegal Moves

  • Moving into check
  • Leaving king in check
  • Castling through check
  • Moving piece in a way it cannot move
  • Touching a piece (touch-move rule in tournaments)

FAMOUS CHESS OPENINGS

Ruy Lopez

Moves: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5

Type: Open Game

Popularity: One of oldest and most analyzed

Named After: 16th century Spanish priest

Strategy: Pressure on e5 pawn, prepare d4

Sicilian Defense

Moves: 1.e4 c5

Type: Semi-Open Game

Popularity: Most popular response to 1.e4

Variations: Najdorf, Dragon, Scheveningen

Strategy: Asymmetric, counter-attacking

Queen's Gambit

Moves: 1.d4 d5 2.c4

Type: Closed Game

Popularity: Extremely popular at all levels

Acceptance: Gambit can be accepted or declined

Strategy: Central control, piece development

History & Origin

  • Modern Rules: 15th century Europe
  • First World Champion: Wilhelm Steinitz (1886)
  • FIDE Founded: 1924
  • Computer Era: Deep Blue beats Kasparov (1997)

Major Championships

  • World Chess Championship: Classical time control
  • World Rapid Championship: 15+10 time control
  • World Blitz Championship: 3+2 time control
  • Chess Olympiad: Team competition every 2 years

MAGNUS CARLSEN

The Mozart of Chess

Nationality: Norwegian

Born: November 30, 1990

Title: Grandmaster (2004)

Peak Rating: 2882 (May 2014)

World Champion: 2013-2023

Playing Style: Universal, endgame specialist

Career Achievements

  • 5-time World Chess Champion (2013, 2014, 2016, 2018, 2021)
  • Highest rated player in history: 2882 Elo (May 2014)
  • Youngest player to reach 2800 Elo: 18 years, 336 days
  • Youngest world number one: 19 years, 32 days
  • Triple Crown: Simultaneous World Champion in Classical, Rapid, and Blitz (2014, 2019)
  • Longest unbeaten streak in classical chess: 125 games (2018-2020)
  • Won Tata Steel Chess Tournament 7 times
  • World Rapid Champion: 2014, 2015, 2019
  • World Blitz Champion: 2009, 2014, 2017, 2018, 2019
  • Known for exceptional endgame skill and converting minimal advantages
